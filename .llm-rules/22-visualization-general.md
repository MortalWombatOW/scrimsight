# 22 - Visualization: General Principles

## Core Principles

- **Purpose First:** Before creating *any* visualization, define its *purpose*.  What question should this visualization answer?  What decision should it support? What action should it prompt?  If a visualization doesn't have a clear purpose, *don't create it*.
- **Audience Awareness:**  Consider the target audience.  Are they technical experts, business users, or the general public?  Adjust the complexity and level of detail accordingly.  Avoid visualizations that try to be "one size fits all."
- **Simplicity:** Strive for simplicity.  Avoid unnecessary complexity, clutter, and "chartjunk" (decorative elements that don't add information).  A simple, clear visualization is more effective than a complex, confusing one.
- **Interactivity (When Appropriate):**  If the visualization is part of an interactive dashboard (like in Scrimsight), consider adding interactive elements (tooltips, filters, drill-downs) to allow users to explore the data.  Don't add interactivity just for the sake of it; it should serve a clear purpose.
- **Accessibility:** Ensure visualizations are accessible to all users, including those with visual impairments.  Use high-contrast color schemes, avoid relying solely on color, and provide alternative text descriptions.
- **Storytelling:**  Use visualizations to tell a story with the data.  Provide context, comparisons, and clear takeaways.  Don't just present raw data; present *insights*.
- **Iterative Design:** Test visualizations with users and iterate based on feedback.  Don't assume your first design is perfect.
- **Consistency:** Maintain consistency in visual style, color palettes, and chart types across the application.
